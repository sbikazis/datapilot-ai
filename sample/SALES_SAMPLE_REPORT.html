What the data suggests

Descriptive observations, not causal explanations
Mix

Apparel leads the sample

Apparel contributes 33.0% of sample net sales. A real analysis should compare this with product availability, promotion periods and unit economics before changing assortment.

Movement

Net sales rise across the six sample months

June is 41.5% above January. Because the values are intentionally generated with a rising factor, this should not be interpreted as a business trend or forecast.

Data quality

Definitions are unusually complete

The fictional file supplies gross sales, discounts, refunds and net sales separately. A customer export may not; definitions and reconciliation should be confirmed before reporting.

Data quality & methodology

Transparent calculation notes
Source profile
  • 24 synthetic order-level records across six months.
  • Four fictional product categories and three fictional regions.
  • One row represents one synthetic order in this sample.
  • Net sales are defined as gross sales − discounts − refunds.
  • Currency is explicitly USD in the generated dataset; no currency inference is used.
Interpretation limits
  • No cost-of-goods or operating expense fields: profit and margin are not calculated.
  • No ad spend or attribution fields: advertising ROI is not calculated.
  • No customer identifier: repeat purchase and customer lifetime value are not calculated.
  • No inventory snapshots: stock availability and lost-sales analysis are not calculated.
  • Small synthetic sample: no statistical generalization, causal claim or forecast.

Questions a real report would resolve

Next-step prompts
  1. Does each row represent an order or an order line, and are cancelled orders excluded?
  2. Are discounts and refunds already deducted from the platform's reported sales total?
  3. Is the date field order date, payment date or fulfillment date?
  4. Are taxes, shipping and duties included in the sales amounts?
  5. Which business decision should the report support this month?
